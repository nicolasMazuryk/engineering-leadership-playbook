# One-on-ones

I hold monthly 1:1s with every direct report as a minimum, and weekly or fortnightly ones during onboarding, incidents and difficult periods. The 1:1 is the report's meeting. It is not a status update.

## Principles

- **The report owns the agenda.** I bring one or two items, not a list.
- **Never skip it.** If I cancel, the message is "you are low priority". Reschedule within the week.
- **Written notes, shared.** A shared document with the date, topics and agreed actions.
- **Feedback early and specific.** Feedback that waits for the review is a failure of the 1:1.
- **Listen first.** Most of the useful information arrives in the last ten minutes, and only if nobody is rushing.

## Rhythm

| Situation | Cadence | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Steady state | Monthly (minimum) | 45-60 min |
| New hire (first 90 days) | Weekly | 30 min |
| Under a performance plan | Weekly | 30 min |
| During a crisis or restructuring | As needed | Short, honest |

## Structure

1. **How are you?** (5 min) Start with the person, not the work.
2. **Their topics** (20 min) Blockers, frustrations, ideas, questions.
3. **Work and priorities** (10 min) Is the load right? Is anything unclear?
4. **Feedback, both ways** (10 min) One thing going well, one thing to change; and ask: "What should I do differently?"
5. **Growth** (10 min) Skills, scope, next level, what they want in a year.
6. **Actions** (5 min) Who does what by when. Written down.

## Question bank

- What is the most annoying thing about your work right now?
- What did you learn last month?
- Where are you spending time that you shouldn't be?
- Is there anything that you are not telling me?
- What would make you consider leaving in the next 12 months?
- What do you want to be doing in a year, and what is in the way?
- Which decision of mine made your life harder recently?

## Signals I watch for

- Short answers where there used to be long ones.
- A sudden rise in sick days or lateness.
- A change in code-review tone.
- Silence about the future.

These do not mean something is wrong. They mean it is time to ask a direct question, in private, with time to listen.

## Hard conversations

- Say the main point in the first sentence.
- Use concrete examples, not labels.
- Separate the facts, the impact and what must change.
- Leave room for their side. I have been wrong about the context more than once.
- Follow up in writing within a day.
