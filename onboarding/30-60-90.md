# 30-60-90 onboarding plan

A template for onboarding an engineer into an existing team. It is genericized from the handover and onboarding notes I use when someone joins a project or takes over an area.

The aim: by day 90, the person owns a real piece of the system, ships without hand-holding, and knows how to find out what they don't know.

## Before day 1

- [ ] Laptop, accounts and repo access are ready on day 1 (not day 3).
- [ ] A named **onboarding buddy**, a peer rather than the manager.
- [ ] A first task chosen, small, real, and shippable in week 1.
- [ ] Calendar holds for the first 1:1s and team ceremonies.
- [ ] Documentation links collected in one place. If the docs are weak, say so honestly.

## Days 1-30: Learn

**Goal:** understand the system, the team and the way we work.

| Area | Outcomes |
|---|---|
| System | Runs the project locally; can explain the architecture in 10 minutes; has read the main decision records |
| Process | Knows the branching, review, release and on-call process; has joined a release |
| Team | 1:1 intro with every teammate and key people in product, QA and ops |
| Delivery | Ships a first small change in week 1 and a few more by day 30 |

**Manager checks:** weekly 1:1; ask "what is confusing?" and write the answers into the docs. Every question a new hire asks is a documentation bug.

## Days 31-60: Contribute

**Goal:** take on real work with moderate guidance.

| Area | Outcomes |
|---|---|
| Delivery | Owns a feature or a bug-fix stream end to end |
| Quality | Reviews others' pull requests; writes tests as part of the work |
| Operations | Handles a support or incident task with the buddy alongside |
| Communication | Gives a short demo or write-up to the team |

**Manager checks:** agree the day-90 goals explicitly; give honest, specific feedback at day 45.

## Days 61-90: Own

**Goal:** operate independently on a defined area.

| Area | Outcomes |
|---|---|
| Ownership | Owns an area (a service, a module, a quality metric) and its roadmap items |
| Judgment | Makes and documents a design decision without being asked |
| Operations | Takes part in the on-call rotation, if the role includes it |
| Improvement | Proposes one improvement to the way the team works |

**Manager checks:** day-90 review against the goals set at day 30. Decide on probation outcome, and write it down.

## Review template (day 30 / 60 / 90)

```
Goals set:        <list>
Evidence so far:  <links, shipped work, feedback>
What went well:   <specific>
What to change:   <specific, with an example>
Support needed:   <from me, buddy, others>
Next checkpoint:  <date>
```

## Handover variant (taking over an area from someone leaving)

Use this when the work is moving from one owner to another.

1. **Inventory:** list services, accounts, schedules, recurring tasks, and who depends on each.
2. **Walkthrough:** the outgoing owner explains each item live while the incoming owner writes it down.
3. **Shadow, then reverse-shadow:** the new owner watches, then drives while the old owner watches.
4. **Written runbook:** every recurring task gets a step-by-step note that someone else could follow.
5. **Open risks list:** things that are fragile, undocumented or owed to someone.
6. **Named cut-over date:** after it, questions go to the new owner.

## Common mistakes

- Treating onboarding as reading documentation for two weeks.
- No real task until week 3.
- Unclear expectations until the first review.
- A buddy with no time set aside.
