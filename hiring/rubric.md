# Engineering interview rubric

Use with the [scorecard in `README.md`](README.md). Each dimension is scored 1-4, with one sentence of evidence.

The examples below describe a **mid-level** engineer at score 3. For senior roles, raise the bar on scope and judgment. For junior roles, lower it on scope and keep it on learning.

## 1. Technical depth

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Cannot explain how their own past systems work |
| 2 | Knows the tools but not why they were chosen; struggles when requirements change |
| 3 | Explains design choices and trade-offs; debugs methodically; writes working, readable code |
| 4 | Anticipates failure modes unprompted; simplifies; has opinions backed by experience |

## 2. Problem solving

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Jumps to a solution without clarifying the problem |
| 2 | Clarifies but gets stuck when the first approach fails |
| 3 | Breaks the problem down, tests assumptions, adjusts when a hint arrives |
| 4 | Reframes the problem, finds a simpler path, and states what they would cut |

## 3. Ownership

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Describes outcomes as things that happened to the team |
| 2 | Owns assigned tasks but waits to be told what is next |
| 3 | Follows work through to production and its consequences; raises risks early |
| 4 | Finds and fixes problems nobody asked them to; others rely on them |

## 4. Communication

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hard to follow; cannot summarize their own work |
| 2 | Clear on technical detail, weak on context or audience |
| 3 | Explains clearly to engineers and to non-engineers; asks good questions |
| 4 | Changes minds with clear writing and clear reasoning; makes the room better |

## 5. Collaboration and feedback

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Dismissive of colleagues; blames others in stories |
| 2 | Works well alone; defensive about feedback |
| 3 | Gives and receives feedback calmly; credits others accurately |
| 4 | Visibly raises the people around them |

## 6. Growth and motivation

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | No view of what they want next |
| 2 | Wants the title, not the scope |
| 3 | Clear direction, learns from mistakes, can name what they changed afterwards |
| 4 | Has a record of deliberate growth and a credible plan |

## Interview questions that work

- "Walk me through the system you are proudest of. What would you change now?"
- "Tell me about a production incident you were part of. What did you do, and what did the system teach you?"
- "Describe a disagreement over a technical decision. How did it end?"
- "What feedback have you received that changed how you work?"
- Practical: "Here is a pull request. Review it as you would for a teammate."

## Red flags and green flags

**Red flags:** blames others consistently; cannot describe their own contribution; contradicts themselves between stages; treats QA or ops as someone else's problem.

**Green flags:** says "I don't know" and then reasons toward an answer; asks about how the team handles failure; names trade-offs without being asked.

## Bias checks

- Compare candidates against the rubric, never against each other.
- Do not score "culture fit" as a feeling. Score the specific behaviors above.
- Notice when a rating rests on accent, writing speed, or the school someone attended. None of those is in the rubric.
